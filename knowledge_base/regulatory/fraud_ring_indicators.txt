COMMON INDICATORS OF COORDINATED INSURANCE FRAUD RINGS

- Multiple claimants sharing the same bank account, device fingerprint,
  mailing address, or broker/agent within a short time window.
- A cluster of claims filed within minutes to hours of each other
  ("claim velocity spike"), especially from previously inactive accounts.
- Inflated claim amounts relative to the claimant's policy history or
  typical claims for the stated incident type.
- Recycled documentation: the same photos, invoice templates, or repair
  estimates appearing across multiple, ostensibly unrelated claims.
- Claimants who cannot be independently verified through public records
  or who share unusual identity attributes (e.g. sequential identifiers,
  identical employer, same notary).
- A broker or agent whose book of business shows a statistically
  anomalous concentration of high-severity claims relative to peers.

When three or more of these indicators co-occur on connected claimants,
the case should be escalated for human review even if no single claim
individually crosses a high-severity threshold.

IMPORTANT CAVEAT: shared infrastructure (a broker, a household address,
an email provider) is common among entirely legitimate claimants and is
not, by itself, evidence of fraud. These indicators describe patterns
that warrant further investigation, not conclusions of wrongdoing.
