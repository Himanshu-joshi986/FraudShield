SUSPICIOUS ACTIVITY REPORT (SAR) FILING REQUIREMENTS

A Suspicious Activity Report must be filed when a financial institution or
insurer detects a transaction or claim involving $5,000 or more that the
institution knows, suspects, or has reason to suspect:

1. Involves funds derived from illegal activity, or is intended to hide
   or disguise funds derived from illegal activity.
2. Is designed to evade reporting or recordkeeping requirements.
3. Has no business or apparent lawful purpose, and the institution knows
   of no reasonable explanation after examining the available facts.
4. Involves the use of the institution to facilitate criminal activity,
   including insurance fraud rings, staged claims, or claim splitting.

SARs must be filed within 30 calendar days of the initial detection of
facts constituting a basis for filing. An additional 30 days is permitted
to identify a suspect. In no case may filing be delayed more than 60 days.

Claim-splitting (structuring a single fraudulent claim into multiple
smaller claims to stay under reporting thresholds) is itself a red flag
requiring escalation regardless of the individual claim amounts.

This document is a general summary for demonstration purposes and does
not constitute legal advice. Actual regulatory requirements vary by
jurisdiction and should be verified against the current text of
applicable law before any real filing decision.
