RESPONSIBLE USE OF AUTOMATED RISK SCORING IN FRAUD INVESTIGATION

Automated risk scores, graph analysis, and machine-learning models are
investigative aids, not determinations of guilt. The following
principles apply to any system that produces a fraud risk score:

1. A risk score indicates that a case warrants review, not that fraud
   has occurred. Language used in case notes and reports should reflect
   this ("risk indicator", "requires review") rather than accusatory
   framing ("this person committed fraud").
2. Automated routing decisions (auto-close, human review, escalation)
   should remain subject to human oversight for medium- and high-risk
   cases, and the specific policy conditions that triggered a routing
   decision should always be recorded and auditable.
3. Model predictions can be affected by dataset bias, distribution
   shift, and incomplete features. A model's confidence should never be
   treated as proof, and disparate impact across demographic groups
   should be periodically reviewed.
4. Individuals affected by an automated fraud determination should have
   a documented path to request human review of that determination.

This document reflects general responsible-AI principles for fraud
investigation systems and is not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific
legal or regulatory compliance requirements.
