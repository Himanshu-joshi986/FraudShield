VEHICLE INSURANCE CLAIM RED FLAGS (NAIC-STYLE GUIDANCE SUMMARY)

Common red flags identified in vehicle insurance fraud investigations
include:

- Claims filed shortly after a policy's inception or a coverage increase.
- Claimant is difficult to reach, or insists on an unusually fast
  settlement or cash payment.
- Police report was not filed for an incident where one would normally
  be expected (e.g. a collision with significant vehicle damage).
- No witnesses are available despite the claimed incident occurring in
  a populated area.
- The claimed damage is inconsistent with the described mechanism of
  loss (e.g. damage pattern does not match the described collision).
- Prior claims history shows an unusually high number of claims over a
  short period, particularly across different insurers.
- The repair shop or medical provider named is one that appears
  disproportionately often across unrelated claims.

These are indicators for further review, consistent with the industry's
general guidance on fraud indicators (e.g. materials published by the
National Insurance Crime Bureau and state insurance regulators). This
summary is for demonstration purposes; consult current official guidance
for actual investigative use.
